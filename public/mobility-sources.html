Walking and terrain

Walking and terrain information is a separate opt-in under Location sharing, off by default, including after upgrading old settings. It is saved for the chosen Mac. Location sharing alone never starts these queries. Your existing Dot can request evidence when a conversation needs it, using a fresh location context and, for a route, a known destination with provenance. No public-transport service is queried and no user account, API key or paid fallback is required.

Your Mac sends exact origin/destination coordinates to FOSSGIS OSRM over HTTPS. FOSSGIS states that route requests are logged; Dot Link does not control that log retention. Terrain requests go to AWS S3's public Mapzen Terrain Tiles dataset and reveal the requested area. Both hosts receive ordinary connection information, including the connecting IP address. No conversation text, destination label, pairing credential or Dot credential is sent to these providers.

Dot receives the requested evidence and its uncertainty through the existing connection: route candidates, model times/distances, endpoint gaps, optional terrain heights, ascent/descent and sampled steep sections. These are not verified access, closures, building/bridge height, accessibility or personalized effort. Ferry segments can appear in the walking graph, but ferry operation and schedules are unknown. Missing data never proves there is no path or that terrain is flat.

One request runs at a time, with no queue. Identical calls can share a request or a memory-only result for at most 30 seconds, bounded further by the original location's freshness. Requests have a 40-second overall limit and provider limits. Terrain rasters remain bounded in memory and are discarded at the end of the request. Dot Link writes no walking queries, destinations or returned route/terrain results to its logs or a disk history. An answer Dot sends may still enter ordinary conversation history, and Dot's own data handling is separate.

Turning this option off rotates/revokes the location-sharing session and cancels pending work on the Mac when the stop arrives. Turning location sharing off, unpairing, app exit, loss of freshness, lease expiry or movement beyond 100 m also prevents old-context results from being used. If the Mac is unreachable, the stop is queued; already-received context can remain usable until the 90-second lease or measurement expires. No stop can retract requests already received by a provider or information already received by Dot. Preferences remain saved for the same Mac; connecting another Mac does not inherit the opt-in.
