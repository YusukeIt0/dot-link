# Why I built Dot Link

In 2025, I was building an app that could understand my preferences and recommend nearby places I might like. That idea eventually became TripSurf.

Around the same time, my rabbit, who had been with me for nearly ten years, passed away.

I started wondering if there was a way to preserve our memories and somehow keep us together. That's when I discovered OpenClaw.

What if I could give OpenClaw memories of my pet and let it take on their personality?

I started experimenting with the idea, trying different approaches, and somehow ended up becoming an OpenClaw contributor along the way.

About a year later, ChatGPT Dot was announced.

My first thought was: What if AI could understand the context of the physical world, almost intuitively, rather than being limited to the digital world?

Something close to what we call Ambient AI. It was also something I had found difficult to achieve with OpenClaw alone.

That's why I built Dot Link.

With Dot and Even G2, a third-party app could provide dynamic location data. But coordinates alone aren't particularly useful. Combine them with maps, weather, terrain, and elevation, and suddenly you can build experiences similar to what I originally envisioned with TripSurf.

I never intended Dot Link to stop at simply letting people use ChatGPT Dot through Even G2.

That's also why I chose to build a foundation that could last, even if response times were slow or a DOM-based bridge would have been easier to implement. Performance can be improved later.

It's also why I could make decisions about what to build and what to leave out without much hesitation, and why development moved unusually fast.

The reason is actually very simple.  
I just wanted to bring the memories of a pet I loved into the digital world and take them with me wherever I go.

Things that seem completely unrelated can suddenly come together as technology evolves. That's why I've come to believe that nothing we experience in life is ever truly wasted.
