# Decision-factor history

Design proposal for [#375](https://github.com/YusukeIt0/dot-link/issues/375), based on [main at f01d086](https://github.com/YusukeIt0/dot-link/tree/f01d0867a2965053da63793e502c246ce9c9cd4e). Dot Link can preserve the evidence that an enabled source already returns. Dot chooses which evidence to read, compare and discuss. A historical read operates entirely on locally retained evidence and makes zero provider requests. Versions, grades, gaps and forecast revisions remain visible so a later answer can describe what was actually known.

## Enrollment and personal control

Existing seven-day grants remain at their original scope and expiry. Live weather or mobility consent does not authorize a persistent archive, even for seven days. Existing consent for an individual factor does not grant disclosure of another factor. Opening a conversation, rebooting, changing a duration or receiving a provider result cannot enroll anyone. History settings must say which factors will be retained and read by the connected Dot, for how long, how to stop and how to delete. The normal reconnect flow needs no history prompt.

The initial investigation compared two UX choices. The single history panel is the direction for implementation. **A single history panel** could show eligible sources, an initially unchecked recording choice for each new archive, and a shared duration. **Controls in each source’s settings** could grant history locally, with a shared view of the effective period and deletion controls. The single panel is the suggested starting point because it makes the effect of changing the period visible in one place. Both choices require affirmative consent for new archives and for retention beyond an existing grant. Authorization remains per source and owner under either presentation.

## One evidence contract

Register each factor with a versioned payload schema, field definitions/units, source attribution, consent and deletion hooks, supported time filters and bounded field projections. Each durable record carries authorized ownership scope, factor and schema/policy version, stable identity and revision, provenance, quality/missingness, source clocks and time precision where supplied, first local availability, retention basis and absolute expiry. Use distinct provenance for measurements, forecasts, provider classifications/models and calculations. A setting change creates a new policy generation. New adapters declare field mappings, dependency guards and the consent scope that covers their evidence. Adding a genuinely new source requires a separate grant. Retired schemas remain readable until expiry or deletion. Unsupported fields return an explicit status.

| Source | Preserve | Recompute or exclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone location and geography | Accepted fixes, accuracy, measured/received times, optional phone time zone/offset, recording boundaries. Attach bounded geographic evidence, map release, provenance, optional distances and ambiguity when originally available. | Generate interval summaries, movement/stay candidates, coverage and phone-clock references from surviving authorized inputs. Map references cannot prove visits or a continuously travelled path. Raw Parquet files, maps and transient IDs stay in their existing lifecycle. |

## Proposed time and expiry semantics

The following is a proposal for validation. On first lawful admission of a distinct record, assign the active retention policy once. Seven days is 168 hours. One and six months and one year add UTC calendar months or a year, preserve the time of day and clamp to the last day of a shorter target month (31 January to 28/29 February, 29 February to 28 February the following non-leap year). Treat expiry as exclusive. Persist the calculated UTC instant so a restart, clock-zone change or repeated pull cannot extend it. A shorter choice applies to **new records only**. Delete covers existing records immediately. A longer choice cannot recover expired data.

For measured observations, use the earlier of the trustworthy measurement instant and first Mac receipt; reject implausible future measurements under the existing source rule. Keep Polar’s conservative earliest possible UTC boundary for offset-free local dates, label it as a retention bound and leave the measured instant unknown. For forecasts and planned routes, propose the first verified local availability of that version as the archive anchor. A target horizon or anticipated arrival cannot prolong retention. Geography attached later cannot outlive its location anchor. A record first received late and already expired under its policy is ineligible. Duplicates preserve the original earliest receipt and expiry. In-place corrections do not renew it. A genuine forecast issue is a distinct version. A gap, missing timestamp or offset supplies no fabricated instant. Retention operates independently of query time axes.

## Historical reads and coverage

One bounded query accepts a time window, factor and field selections, summary/detail, and an optional knowledge cutoff. Filter measurements by source time where known, forecast horizons by validity and versions by known-at time, dates by possible overlap, and plans by when they became available. Return the clock basis, partial overlap, units, source notices, version and coverage for the selected evidence. A cursor binds the same owner, filters, ordering and authorization generation. Limit output, inspected rows and elapsed work for summaries as well as details. Missing fields, partial provider data, stopped sharing, expiry, pressure-related omissions and absent evidence remain distinguishable where the store has evidence of the cause. A blank result alone cannot establish what the person did.

Check each source’s authorization at read, cursor continuation and final delivery. A historical read requires no fresh phone fix. Follow the existing question and utterance guards on G2 and join-source guards for a combined answer. Short-lived access receipts remain ephemeral. A read never enables recording, sends a notification or grants proactive disclosure. ## Protection, deletion and compatibility

Use one query contract over source-owned storage. Retain the existing authenticated health encryption, and protect new archives of private location, weather and movement along with their indexes. Limit any common index to information safe outside a source vault. Health dates, category occupancy, values, counts and join keys must remain inside its protected boundary. Encrypted segments with bounded indexes merit measurement before selecting an on-disk format. The benchmark’s unencrypted SQLite indexes model query shape only.

Cap and fence ingress, reads and replies by owner, source, consent generation and expiry. For OFF, deletion, revocation, account changes and disconnect, respect the existing source-specific lifecycle. On explicit deletion, first durably invalidate the relevant generation and prior acquisition boundary, then erase its payloads, linked derivatives, indexes, caches and receipts. Failed cleanup remains inaccessible and resumes after restart. Reject stale in-flight callbacks and rereads that would repopulate deleted records. A migration carries original scope, source precision and expiry; import only still-authorized records. Unsupported older software must fail closed or leave the new store unopened. Replaying an old file or journal must fail when the independent deletion/key generation has advanced. A full-system restore can roll back both storage and local authority; document that limit and require safe recovery before claiming protection across such restores. Local deletion does not retract conversations already delivered to Dot or guarantee erasure of external backups.

## Capacity and validation

One SQLite model held 1,021,635 synthetic rows in 381.3 MiB. At its assumed 1,920 fixes/day, the existing 50,000-fix cap spans 26 days. A two-factor projected read for 100 fixes and 100 forecast rows took 1.90 ms p95 on the development Mac. Actual production storage primitives also ran against synthetic inputs. The current health vault rejected a 28,172,327-byte year of five observations/day with 1,440 activity-zone samples/day, despite staying below 4,000 observations. Production location startup rebuilt 13,440 fixes in 455 ms p95. These findings support bounded time-slice reads and independently encrypted health partitions. An advertised year needs measurements of encrypted, representative records, storage pressure, concurrent writers and cold start on the target Mac. Show actual coverage when capacity prevents storage.

Before shipment, exercise controlled-clock transitions, leap dates, admission of late results, revisions, cursor invalidation, pressure, migration/rollback, deletion and revocation races. A test-only added/retired field or adapter must leave older authorized evidence readable. Validate actual Dot selection, comparison, follow-up detail and no/partial coverage separately from synthetic queries, then check affected client and G2 behavior. Reconcile consent, privacy, attribution, supported-language UI and relevant distribution declarations with shipped behavior. This design and the measurements do not establish implementation or device acceptance.

## Synthetic measurement evidence

### One-year synthetic sizing

A single local SQLite model stored **1,021,635 synthetic evidence records in 399,781,888 bytes (381.3 MiB)**. The experiment assumes a 365-day collection year, one owner and all five factors enabled. The rates describe one reproducible workload. Actual retention volume depends on how often the phone supplies fixes, how many providers return new evidence, the returned fields and how often Dot requests a route.

| Factor | Assumed input | Annual records | Mean payload bytes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Location | 8 active hours/day, one distinct accepted fix per 15 seconds. The current native request is 15 seconds with a 25 m distance filter; stationary periods or background restrictions can reduce actual delivery. | 700,800 | 145 |
| Geography | Bounded evidence attached to one quarter of fixes, represented as a separate linked-time row with one area and one line. | 175,200 | 389 |
| Health | One synthetic observation per currently supported Polar category per day. Model uses opaque payload lengths with a 28-byte per-record allowance. | 1,825 | 205 |
| Weather | Eight distinct synthetic forecast issues/day, each with 49 hourly horizons and one precipitation period. Separate validity and availability clocks preserve revisions. A real unchanged fetch should introduce no duplicate forecast. | 143,080 | 352 |
| Mobility | Two requested route models/day with 24 steps and 64 terrain points each. No route acquisition was performed. | 730 | 2,611 |

Every row has a stable digest, validity/observation time, availability time and an expiry column; SQLite indexes the digest, factor/time/ID and expiry. The fixed 365-day offset populates the benchmark expiry index only. Production retention needs its own calendar policy. Geographic evidence completeness, forecast issue frequency, payload packing and route geometry will change final database size. A route response can currently reach 128 KiB; two near-limit responses/day alone could contribute 91.3 MiB/year before database overhead.

The current 50,000-point location cap holds 26.0 active days at this workload, or 8.7 days at uninterrupted 15-second delivery. The current 4,000-record health cap fits this five-per-day year; at 20 distinct records/day it holds 200 days. Both current implementations also enforce seven days. Extending retention requires per-record expiry and explicit capacity-omission metadata. A one-year setting cannot inherit the current caps without tracking when they shorten the stored coverage.

| Query on populated database | p50 | p95 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One location field from 100 records at a cursor four days into a seven-day window | 0.720 ms | 0.819 ms |
| Two-factor comparison, one field from each of 100 location and 100 forecast records in the requested window | 1.280 ms | 1.902 ms |
| Daily location counts over 31 days (59,520 observations, 31 result rows) | 6.354 ms | 6.745 ms |

The script varies requested windows across the year, discards three preliminary runs and records 101 warm-process trials. SQLite used the factor/time index for pagination. Bulk generation and insertion took 36.15 seconds with one transaction per synthetic day. The measurements cover an arm64 development Mac using Python 3.13.17 and SQLite 3.54.1, with an 8 MiB SQLite cache and ordinary OS file caching. Temporary databases are removed after the run. These figures measure the specified model; production encryption, per-record commits, provider APIs, concurrent access, cold starts, backup/journal space and Mac mini latency require separate implementation-specific checks. No private history or service was used. The plaintext index of this synthetic fixture must not be copied into production for health metadata.

Integration should retain bounded, indexed cursor pagination and enforce hard limits on the number of records inspected by each aggregation. The year-level capacity measurement supports prototyping this layout. It gives no basis to increase a source's sampling rate or to enable an archive before its storage rights are established.

### Current storage implementations under synthetic load

The measured fixture calls the existing `HealthVault.write/read` and `LocationHistoryStore` on a temporary directory and removes it in a `finally` block. It uses a disposable 32-byte key. Measurements below used arm64 Node v26.12.1. Three warm-ups precede 25 timed runs, except the location rehydration measurement (nine timed runs).

| Health history | Per-day activity-zone detail | Encrypted snapshot bytes | Whole write p50 / p95, ms | Whole read p50 / p95, ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 days × 5 records | 0 samples | 11,073 | 0.291 / 0.616 | 0.096 / 0.107 |
| 365 days × 5 records | 0 samples | 568,837 | 1.154 / 3.605 | 1.177 / 1.318 |
| 7 days × 5 records | 96 samples | 46,535 | 0.281 / 0.439 | 0.195 / 0.285 |
| 365 days × 5 records | 96 samples | 2,417,927 | 3.990 / 7.980 | 5.954 / 9.725 |
| 365 days × 5 records | 1,440 samples | 28,172,327 required | Refused by the existing 8 MiB limit | — |

The fixture generates one synthetic record per supported category/day. The zone-detail stress case remains within the existing schema's 2,500 samples per activity record. It demonstrates that a one-year archive can exceed the vault limit with only 1,825 observations, well below the separate 4,000-observation cap. The one-year inputs were submitted directly to the real AES-256-GCM storage primitive to isolate whole-snapshot cost. The existing `HealthContext` still enforces seven-day expiry. The timings include JSON serialization/parsing, encryption/authentication and local file I/O. They exclude Keychain access, provider acquisition, schema validation and production data distributions. Retain separate encrypted health storage, and assess bounded encrypted segments or equivalent partitioning before offering a one-year history.

For location, the fixture seeded 13,440 schema-compatible synthetic fixes (seven days at 1,920 fixes/day) into a database initialized by the production store. No geographic payloads were attached. Production startup read and rebuilt the in-memory history index from a 6,856,704-byte database in **432.856 ms p50 / 455.152 ms p95**. A production one-hour detail read returning 50 observations took **7.144 ms p50 / 8.127 ms p95**. Seeding uses one SQL transaction, so no production ingestion timing is claimed. The current reader enforces seven days, and startup restores at most 50,000 rows. A longer-retention implementation should retrieve indexed time slices from storage and avoid eagerly rehydrating a whole year of location fixes. The separate one-year model measures a different query shape and workload.
